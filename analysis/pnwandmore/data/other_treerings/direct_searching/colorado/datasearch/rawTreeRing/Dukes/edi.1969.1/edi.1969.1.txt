         This package includes tree growth-ring widths for increment cores collected from a long-term 'macroplot' established in old-growth hemlock-northern hardwoods forest at the Dukes Research Natural Area/Dukes Experimental Forest in the Hiawatha National Forest in Marquette Co., MI. Tree demographic monitoring data for the entire ca. 3.0 ha macroplot, from 1992 to 2019, are available in the EDI package edi.1526.1. In 1993, 1994 and 1995, increment cores were taken for all 'core-able' trees greater than ~ 10 cm diameter for a subsection of the macroplot about 1 ha in area. Trees that were obviously badly rotten and hollow or steeply leaning were not cored. Cores are not cross-dated. See Methods for more details. This data-package may be cross-referenced to the demographic data in edi.1526.1 using stem numbers.

               Field Methods: Over an approximately 1-ha portion of the macroplot described in package edi.1526.1, all suitable trees > 10 cm in diameter were cored in the summers of 1993, 1994, and 1995. Trees that were obviously substantially hollow or rotten, leaning heavily, or otherwise significantly compromised were skipped. A single core was taken at a height of about 1.2 m, from south to north (except as otherwise noted in the 'field notes' field of the core information data-table). Where rot or hollowness prevented taking a complete core, only the 'south-facing' section is included (indicated by "a" in the 'side' code in data-set).  Where a single complete core (bark to bark) could be extracted, both sides ("a" and 'north-facing' "b" side) are included.  Ring measurements and further information for 232 trees are included here.
            
         
         
            
               Lab Methods: Cores were mounted on wooden blocks and sanded. Rings were measured, in the ecology lab at Bennington College, for all cores during 1994-1997, using "Project J2X" software and a Velmex ring measurement system.  Cross-dating proved to be impractical, and data in this package are raw measurements (not cross-dated). Maximum ring-count was 299.
