###########Brief intro
This folder contains tree ring data collected in (or very near) Mount Rainier National Park for Mountain Hemlock (TSME), Subalpine Fir (ABLA) and Douglas fir (PSME). Data were collected by Lisa Graumlich and Linda Brubaker in the late 70's/ early 80's and by David L Peterson and David W Peterson in 1992. There are several sites for each species. I am nearly 100% sure this represents raw ring widths (not standardized) for individual trees (but probably worth checking). 

Note that are many other tree ring data series collected across their ranges (including in similar mountains / climates and nearby locations - e.g. Olympic national park, Mt. Adams, mt. Baker). This is especially the case for TSME, ABLA, and PSME. It could be interesting to include these data in a separate analysis to assess climate - growth relationships at range wide scales and perhaps to ask the extent to which dendro data are collected from trees with much greater climate sensitivity. See ITRDB data and / or papers by Dave Peterson (and students) including Jeremy Littell, Christina Restaino. Also data collected by Linda Brubaker and her students (perhaps also Andy Bunn???).

#######Where is the ring width data?
Look inside the folders called Graumlich... and Peterson... for the ringwidths folders. The Other ITRDB data folders include everything else from ITRDB (most not useful).

##########Where the data in this folder came from 
JHRL downloaded the data mid March from the ITRDB website (see website below - search for tree rings). https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/paleo-search/?dataTypeId=18

##########Publications that are relevant to this data
- Peterson and Peterson 2001; 2002 - these are the publications that present the ABLA and TSME tree ring data. Note these studies address much larger scales (Mt. Rainier is just one of many locations).
- Graumlich and Brubaker 1986 - I think this publication used some of the data posted by Graumlich (the upper elevation TSME). I'm not sure where the PSME tree ring data was published (if at all); perhaps only in the dissertation (which is available via UW).
- Zhao 2019 - this article discusses some of the issues with the ITRDB database, and importantly, links to a harmonized tree ring database (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/paleo-search/study/25570). It may be worth re downloading the data from this database - it could be in a better format.
- Summer Jennings 2021 - Clearly this study generated more relevant tree ring data from Mt. Rainier, but I could not find it posted in ITRDB or via the publication (worth an email exchange if we are interested).
